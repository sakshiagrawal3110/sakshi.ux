When should we trust AI?

A UX research study exploring how people decide when to trust, verify, or reject AI-generated information.

Exploring the behavioural factors behind AI trust - and how transparency, evidence, uncertainty and user control can make AI experiences feel safer and more understandable.

Role & Focus

UX Researcher · Product Designer

Focus: UX Research, AI UX, Behavioural Research, Trust, Human-in-the-loop.

Methods & Tools

Mixed Methods Research

User Interviews, Diary Study, Usability Testing, Thematic Analysis.
Tools: FigJam, Figma, UserTesting.

01 - The Research Question

AI can give us an answer in seconds. But what makes us believe it?

AI products are increasingly capable of generating answers, summaries, recommendations and decisions. But capability doesn't automatically create trust.

Users may:

  • accept an AI answer without checking it
  • verify information they don't fully understand
  • reject an answer even when it is correct
  • trust confident language more than evidence
  • become uncomfortable when they don't understand how AI reached an answer

Research question

What makes users trust, question, or reject AI-generated information?

02 - Research Hypothesis

Framing the assumption

Rather than starting with: "Users don't trust AI."

I framed the hypothesis more carefully: Trust may not come from making AI appear more confident. It may come from making AI more understandable and controllable.

This becomes something you test rather than assume.

03 - Research Plan

A structured framework for understanding trust

04 - Participant Profile

Recruiting across the spectrum

I recruited 12 participants split across three behavioral archetypes to capture a diversity of mental models:

  • AI Explorers: People who regularly use ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude etc.
  • AI Pragmatists: People who use AI occasionally for specific tasks.
  • AI Skeptics: People who actively question or avoid AI-generated information.
05 - Interview Approach

Behaviour > Opinion

Instead of asking: "Do you trust AI?"

I asked: "Tell me about the last time an AI tool gave you an answer you weren't sure about."

This allowed me to probe into their actual behavior: What made them question it? Did they verify it? Where? What would have made them more confident? This ensures we are documenting observed behavior rather than stated opinions.

06 - Diary Study

Trust happens in moments, not surveys.

Participants documented interactions with AI for 5–7 days. We captured context, the AI output, their reaction, their verification steps, and their ultimate decision.

The interesting insight isn't simply "the user didn't trust AI." It's: AI was trusted as a starting point, but not as evidence.

07 - Usability Study

Testing interface variables

I created three versions of the same AI response to test exactly what drives confidence and action.

  • Version A: Answer only.
  • Version B: Answer + explanation.
  • Version C: Answer + evidence + control.

Participants were asked to evaluate the recommendation, while we measured confidence, willingness to act, and perceived transparency.

08 - The Synthesis

Finding the patterns

Interview transcripts and diary logs were synthesized into an affinity map, organizing observations into core themes: Understanding, Verification, Control, Uncertainty, and Responsibility.

09 - Key Research Insights

What we learned

1. Trust isn't binary.

Users may trust AI for Discovery → Organisation → Summarisation, but hesitate when AI moves toward Recommendation → Decision → Action.

2. Confidence isn't the same as credibility.

A polished, confident response can feel convincing. But users become more comfortable when they can understand: Where did this come from? What information was used? Can I verify it?

3. Users don't necessarily want more explanation.

They want the right explanation at the right moment. Instead of exposing technical AI reasoning, surface useful evidence.

4. Control is a trust mechanism.

Trust increases when users can Inspect → Modify → Verify → Reject, rather than simply Accept → Continue.

10 - The Trust Spectrum

Mapping the interaction model

AI interaction isn't simply "Trust AI" or "Don't trust AI". It exists on a spectrum. Different levels of risk require different levels of user control.

11 - Opportunity Areas & Principles

From Research to Product

How do we turn these research insights into tangible product opportunities? We move from finding facts to defining design principles.

12 - Conceptual Direction

AI should assist the decision, not silently make it

If we apply these principles to a UI, the interface shifts. Instead of the AI simply saying "This is the best option," the AI explains the parameters, provides supporting evidence, and allows the user to adjust the criteria. It doesn't ask the user to trust it blindly-it helps the user evaluate it.

Outcome

A framework for safer AI experiences

The research framework turns AI trust into a product design problem. It helps teams identify when users need explanations, when evidence should be surfaced, when AI should communicate uncertainty, and when AI should step back and let humans decide.

"The goal isn't to make users trust AI more. It's to help users know when AI deserves their trust."

Researching AI Trust: The Short Version

  • The Objective: Conduct mixed-methods research to understand what makes users trust, verify, or reject AI-generated information.
  • The Process: Conducted User Interviews, a 5-day Diary Study, and Usability Testing (A/B/C variants) with 12 participants spanning AI Explorers to AI Skeptics.
  • The Insight: Trust is not binary. Users readily trust AI for discovery, but demand evidence, control, and verifiable sources for decision-making. Confidence does not equal credibility.

Evidence > Confidence

Polished AI prose triggers skepticism. Verifiable source nodes increase trust.

The Trust Spectrum

Mapped user behavior from passive acceptance to active rejection based on risk.

Control is Crucial

Trust spikes when users are given an "escape hatch" to edit or modify AI output.

Honest Uncertainty

Users prefer AI that clearly communicates its limitations over false precision.
