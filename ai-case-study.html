AI Clinical Documentation

Designing AI to reduce clinical documentation without taking control away from clinicians

A conceptual exploration of how AI could help clinicians turn patient consultations into structured clinical notes while maintaining transparency, human oversight, accessibility and trust.

My Role

Product Designer

Responsible for the end-to-end UX concept, including workflow definition, information architecture, AI interaction design, wireframing, prototyping and accessibility considerations.

Impact Highlights

Reduced administrative effort

Human oversight built into the workflow, highly traceable outputs, and AI designed fundamentally around trust.

The Challenges

Balancing Attention and Trust

Documentation competes with patient attention

Clinicians need to listen, ask questions, make decisions and maintain a human interaction while simultaneously capturing information for the clinical record.

AI introduces a trust problem

A healthcare professional cannot simply accept an AI-generated answer because it sounds plausible. They need to understand what the AI generated, what information it used and whether anything has been misunderstood.

Automation has a boundary

The opportunity wasn't to automate clinical judgement. It was to automate repetitive documentation tasks while keeping interpretation, diagnosis and final approval with the clinician.

The Audit & Role

Starting with the clinical workflow

Rather than beginning with the question "What can AI do?", I mapped where clinicians spend effort throughout a consultation. The workflow revealed an opportunity to separate documentation effort from clinical decision-making.

The goal wasn't to automate the entire workflow. It was to identify where AI could remove repetitive work while leaving clinical responsibility with the professional.

The assistant was intentionally limited to four core capabilities: Capture (convert to transcript), Organise (identify relevant info), Summarise (draft a note), and Retrieve. The AI does not independently diagnose, prescribe or make treatment decisions.

Interaction

Making AI-generated information distinguishable

One of the core design decisions was to visually separate three different types of information: what the patient actually said, what the system generated or inferred, and information that has been reviewed and accepted by the clinician.

This distinction reduces the risk of generated information being mistaken for verified clinical information.

Verification

AI proposes. The clinician decides.

Instead of presenting an AI-generated note as a finished answer, each important piece of information can be traced back to the consultation. This gives clinicians a way to verify information rather than relying on AI confidence alone.

The review experience was designed around explicit user control. Each AI-generated item can be accepted, edited, or removed. This creates a human-in-the-loop workflow: Generate → Review → Verify → Approve rather than automatically saving.

Handling Constraints

Don't hide uncertainty behind a polished answer

The system should make uncertainty visible. If information is missing, contradictory, or unclear, the interface provides clear pathways to review it manually or check the original transcript. Rather than pretending the system is always correct, the interface makes uncertainty actionable.

Accessibility matters at every step

The experience was designed around clear visual hierarchy, keyboard accessibility, readable transcription, and ensuring that color isn't the only signal used to communicate AI states and warnings.

Outcome

A model for responsible AI in clinical workflows

The concept demonstrates how AI could be introduced into a high-stakes healthcare workflow without turning the clinician into a passive reviewer.

The result is not an AI that makes clinical decisions. It is an AI that helps clinicians spend less time documenting and more time reviewing, reasoning and caring for the patient.

"AI doesn't need to replace the clinician to be useful. It just needs to make the clinician's work easier - without taking away their control."

Designing AI for Healthcare: The Short Version

  • The Challenge: Clinicians suffer from documentation fatigue, but AI creates a trust problem. Doctors cannot simply accept AI-generated notes without verifying context and accuracy.
  • The Strategy: Separate documentation from decision-making. The AI handles capturing, organizing, and summarizing, while the clinician retains full authority over diagnosis and approval.
  • The Solution: A transparent workflow where AI-generated content is visually distinct, easily traceable back to the source transcript, and requires explicit human review before entering the clinical record.
